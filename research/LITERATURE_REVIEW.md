# Literature review and gap identification

**Review date:** 2026-10-14  
**Scope:** NIFTY 50 / Indian equities forecasting, general financial forecast evaluation, and data-source documentation. Papers are evidence about prior study designs, not evidence for this project's model performance.

## Synthesis

The NIFTY-specific literature is rich in comparisons among linear regression, ARIMA, SVMs, random forests, XGBoost, CNNs and LSTMs. Results are highly dependent on target definition, time period, feature set and split design. Many studies forecast price levels whose strong persistence can make R-squared misleadingly large. A separate evaluation of returns, direction and volatility against simple benchmarks is therefore essential.

An October 2026 Cureus Journal of Computer Science article by Sain and Singh compares 12 supervised regressors with a naïve persistence forecast on Yahoo Finance NIFTY 50 data, using 5-, 10- and 20-year windows and chronological splits. It reports strong linear-model results and weaker extrapolation by tree ensembles on longer windows. Its discussion itself recommends aligned out-of-sample periods, rolling-origin evaluation, significance testing and shifting attention from raw price levels to returns/direction. This is an especially relevant nearby study, but its different test windows, target definitions and single split mean its reported metric values are not pooled with this project's results.

Mehtab, Sen and Dutta (2021 / preprint 2020) study machine learning and LSTM methods for NIFTY open-price forecasts and report a univariate LSTM advantage under their walk-forward setting. Mehtab and Sen's related CNN/LSTM work uses weekly multi-step forecasts. Fathali et al. (2022) examine ML techniques on NIFTY 50. Such papers motivate including sequence models, but model rankings across separate datasets cannot identify a universal winner.

Broader forecast-evaluation research stresses correct comparison with benchmarks, forecast-error dependence, nested-model effects, and multiple testing. Diebold and Mariano provide a general predictive-accuracy test; Clark and West provide an adjusted test suited to comparisons where a larger model nests a smaller benchmark; White's Reality Check frames the danger of selecting the best result from a large specification search. The probability-of-backtest-overfitting literature likewise warns that reuse of one historical sample for search and confirmation inflates apparent performance. While this research is prediction-only (not a trading backtest), the data-snooping lesson applies to model/horizon/feature selection.

NSE documents that NIFTY 50 is an NSE Indices-managed free-float index and that India VIX is calculated from the best bid/ask prices of NIFTY options to express expected near-term volatility over roughly 30 calendar days. Dhan's documentation describes daily historical OHLC data from instrument inception and intraday OHLC windows up to five years, with supported candle intervals. These are source capability statements; actual access, coverage and license conditions must be tested in the Actions runtime.

### Excluded uploaded papers outside prediction-only scope

The uploaded documents titled "Options Trading Strategy: A Quantitative Study from an Investor's POV" (D0801051829.pdf), "An Empirical Study on Options Trading Strategy Using Commodity Channel Index For NSE's Nifty Options in India" (ssrn-3323746.pdf), and "Developing a Machine Learning-Based Options Trading Strategy for the Indian Market" (50375.pdf) primarily concern trading rules, options P&L, strategy performance or portfolio metrics. They are excluded from the prediction scoreboard because the current user-defined scope is forecasting only. Their returns, Sharpe ratios and trading-strategy "accuracy" are not imported as evidence of index forecast skill.

### Cross-paper inference boundary

Across the uploaded papers, claims of 83–99% "accuracy" can refer to normalized continuous-price forecasts, closeness of level predictions, or directional labels with varying definitions. Different periods and splits also confound rankings. The current study therefore uses these papers as candidates/hypotheses and ranks models only using same-target, same-horizon, same-chronological-holdout comparisons against suitable baselines in this repository.

## Gaps the study addresses

1. Use aligned split boundaries across all models within each target/horizon; compare return and direction separately from levels.
2. Preserve one untouched final chronological test block after development-model selection.
3. Treat publication times and global-market trading hours as part of feature eligibility, not a simple date join.
4. Quantify whether feature groups add holdout value, not just whether a large fitted model has high train metrics.
5. Report calibration, sample sizes, dependence-aware confidence intervals and corrected significance.
6. Make data presence / absence explicit, including options, FII/DII, market breadth and news sentiment.
7. Preserve all catalogue exclusions and failures so the final claim is commensurate with evidence.
